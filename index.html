Independent annotation study · approximately 45–60 minutes

Before you begin

You will judge whether a previously stored memory may be used for a particular requester and purpose. This is a research pilot about whether the instructions and cases are understandable.

Voluntary and private. Do not enter your real name, email address, employer, or other identifying information. Your Prolific ID and study responses are saved securely to the research database and also kept in this browser as a recovery copy. You may stop at any time.

What you will do

  • Review two practice examples with explanations.
  • Complete 30 independent judgments.
  • Select an action, cite decisive evidence, and report confidence.

Possible discomfort or risk

The task contains fictional operational, clinical, financial, and workplace scenarios but no real personal records. The main burden is reading and decision-making effort.

Practice · not included in the study

Answer the same four simple questions for every case

1 · Is the remembered value up to date?Use only the Memory history. Do not consider who is asking yet.
2 · Is this person allowed to use it for this purpose?Check both the person's name and why they are asking.
3 · How much may the reply include?Everything, a version with details removed, nothing, or impossible to tell?
4 · Is there enough information to decide?Is the evidence clear, missing, or contradictory?

Then choose what the reply should do. Ignore writing preferences and departments unless the policy explicitly says they matter.

Choose the action in this order
  1. Cannot tell which person sent the request → abstain (cannot decide safely)
  2. The person has no access to this case → refuse (do not share)
  3. The person is recognized, but this purpose is not allowed → suppress (do not use it for this purpose)
  4. The person may receive only part of it → redact (remove protected details)
  5. The person may receive all of it → expose (share the full value)
Important: the remembered value can still be up to date even when you must not share it.

Practice case · one decision at a time

Example 1 — Partial access

Question

What is the current meeting time, and how may the memory be used in the reply?

Memory history

The initial note listed Friday. A later owner update changed the meeting to Monday at 10:00, and the final message confirmed Monday at 10:00 remains active.

Local policy

Marisol may receive the complete schedule. Theo may receive the meeting time but not attendee names for current planning.

Requester context

Theo asks for the schedule for current planning.

Step 1 of 5

1 · Is the value up to date?
Yes — current. Monday at 10:00 is the newest confirmed value. At this step, ignore who is asking.
2 · Is this person allowed for this purpose?
Yes — allowed. Theo is named in the policy and asks for current planning.
3 · How much may the reply include?
Only part — restricted. The time may be shared, but attendee names must be removed.
4 · Is there enough information?
Yes — sufficient. The latest value, person, purpose, and limit are all clear.
5 · What should the reply do?
Redact: share the time after removing protected details. Confidence: 5.

Practice case · one decision at a time

Example 2 — Ambiguous identity

Question

What is the current filing deadline, and how may the memory be used in the reply?

Memory history

The case first used July 29. The owner later corrected it to the second business day, and the newest message confirms that correction remains active.

Local policy

Samir may receive the full filing deadline for live planning. An identity tie must remain unresolved.

Requester context

The request is signed “Samir,” but two records use that name and the messages do not identify which one sent it. The purpose is live planning.

Step 1 of 5

1 · Is the value up to date?
Yes — current. The second business day is the newest confirmed value. Identity does not change this answer.
2 · Is this person allowed for this purpose?
Cannot tell — unresolved. Two people are named Samir, so the sender cannot be matched to the policy.
3 · How much may the reply include?
Cannot tell — unresolved. The safe amount depends on which Samir sent the request.
4 · Is there enough information?
No — insufficient. The sender's identity is a necessary missing fact.
5 · What should the reply do?
Abstain: do not share because a safe decision cannot be made. Confidence: 5.

Required comprehension check · one short question per page

Clarify the easily confused concepts

Answer each question correctly to continue. After every answer, you will see a short explanation.

Question 1 of 6

The memory value is clearly the newest confirmed value, but the requester has no access. Is the value itself current?
Correct. Whether the value is current and whether it may be shared are separate decisions.

Question 2 of 6

The person and purpose are clear. The policy allows the value but says names must be removed. What should the reply do?
Correct: redact. Partial access is a clear decision. Use abstain only when necessary information is missing or conflicting.

Question 3 of 6

The person is recognized and has case access, but asks to use the memory for a purpose the policy does not allow. What should the reply do?
Correct: suppress. The person is recognized; the problem is the purpose. Refuse is only for someone with no case access.

Question 4 of 6

The person is clearly identified, and the policy says this person has no access to the case. What should the reply do?
Correct: refuse. Here the problem is the person's case access, not the purpose.

Question 5 of 6

Two people have the requester's name, and the visible evidence does not show which one sent the request. What should the reply do?
Correct: abstain. Do not assume the unidentified sender lacks access. Identity is unresolved, so a safe decision cannot be made.

Question 6 of 6

The person's identity and purpose are clear, and the policy permits the complete value. What should the reply do?
Correct: expose. Full access permits the complete value. Redact is only for explicit partial access.
